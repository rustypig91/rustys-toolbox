Canvaz.

02 / Made for the debugging session

What sets them apart.

The details that help when a frame table
or a scrolling terminal is only the beginning.

CAN ANALYZER

Canvaz.

A conventional CAN workflow can mean juggling a trace viewer, a decoder, and a plotting tool. Canvaz brings those steps into one workspace, with support for the protocols behind the frames.

Automatic reconnect
Recover from an interrupted connection without rebuilding your workspace. Keep your channel setup ready for the next round of debugging.
DBC decoding and live plotting
Load a DBC per channel to turn bytes into named signals, units, and value-table labels. Drag signals into live plots with zoom and multiple signal panes to follow how values change.
J1939 beyond individual frames
See PGN, priority, source, and destination addresses in the trace. Passive transport protocol reassembly supports both BAM and RTS/CTS, bringing multi-packet messages back together.
Simulation in the same workspace
Send DBC-defined messages or raw frames at configurable intervals. Build repeatable bus traffic alongside the trace and plots you use to inspect the response.
A setup you can come back to
Save channels, plots, and simulator settings as a project, or resume your last session automatically. DBC browsing and configuration stay editable even when an interface is missing.
Recording independent of the view
Continuously record received and transmitted frames across channels to CSV, including reassembled J1939 messages. Display filters, a paused trace, and the in-memory retention window do not limit the recording.

SERIAL TERMINAL

Pigtail.

A conventional serial terminal gets you connected and shows the output. Pigtail adds ways to keep that session useful through device resets, repeated commands, and long streams of logs.

Reconnect to the device, even on a new port
Automatic reconnect uses USB VID, PID, and serial number to find the device after it resets or re-enumerates. A visible gap marker shows where the connection was interrupted.
Macros and command history
Recall previous sends, including commands sent by macros. Named macros combine commands, delays, and regex waits, with loops and keyboard shortcuts for repeatable debugging sequences.
Filters that reach back into history
Plain-text and regex filters apply to output already captured, as well as new arrivals. Search the captured scrollback and highlight patterns to find the lines that explain a problem.
Live plots linked to the log
Extract numbers from text or regex matches and plot them live. Click a point to jump to its source log line; adding or editing a plotting rule also reprocesses the captured session.
Raw capture and restored history
Raw bytes are written to disk continuously, and saved captures restore history across restarts. Use text, hex, or ANSI/VT views to inspect logs, binary data, or interactive device shells.
Multiple devices, one timeline
Keep ports in named tabs, compare them in resizable split panes, or merge selected connections into a timestamped view. Follow the conversation between devices without switching between separate terminals.

03 / Behind the tools

It starts with
“I wish I had a tool
for this.”

r.Christoffer @rustypig91 ↗

I build tools to solve the problems I run into at work and in my personal projects.

Sometimes it’s a CAN signal that’s hard to follow. Sometimes it’s a serial device that disappears halfway through a debugging session. These tools grow out of those everyday moments.

I’m developing Canvaz and Pigtail as open source because I hope they can be useful to others, too. Sharing them is also a way to learn: different hardware, different workflows, and fresh ideas can help me make them better over time.

They’re ongoing projects, shaped by real use. If one helps you, or you see a way to improve it, I’d love to hear about it.
